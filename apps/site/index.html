Address poisoning / Account enforcement

A familiar address.
A different recipient.

Address poisoning puts lookalike addresses into your transaction history. A tiny or zero-value transfer can make the wrong recipient look familiar when you copy an address for your next payment.

HexFence connects suspected patterns with a payment check — and an on-chain rule for approved payees.

From observation to enforcement

A warning is only part of the story.

The evidence informs the client. The Guard enforces its own resemblance rule on-chain.

  1. Observe →

    Detector

    Reads Ethereum mainnet USDT and USDC transfer logs for address-poisoning patterns.

  2. Publish →

    Arkiv evidence

    Publishes open records on the Rabdi testnet. Every record has status suspected, not a finding about a person.

  3. Check →

    HexFence Pay verdict

    Checks the recipient locally against approved payees and bulk-read suspected evidence before you sign.

  4. Enforce ↳

    HexFenceGuard

    When active on the Safe, refuses a supported payment to a different address sharing an approved payee’s fingerprint. A public listing alone does not trigger this rule.

A defined boundary

Know what it covers.

This is a testnet payment demonstration, not universal protection.

  • Only supported payments are inspected. ETH payments and token transfer / transferFrom calls are covered. Other calls are allowed by the Guard; the client shows “cannot verify”.
  • Account paths matter. Module transactions bypass transaction guards. MultiSend uses delegatecall and is refused, rather than inspected.
  • Resemblance has a boundary. The fingerprint compares the beginning and end of an address. Weaker lookalikes are not covered.
  • Evidence is partial and suspected. The detector covers Ethereum mainnet USDT and USDC only. An absent record is not an endorsement. Human-labelled precision is still pending.
  • The demonstration has limits. The recorded payment flow used a scripted wallet. The human wallet check remains pending. A broken Guard can lock a Safe; use an isolated testnet account and test funds.
